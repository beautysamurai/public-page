**要約:** 価格決定者が粗視化された注文フロー信号で posterior\-mean pricing を行い、より細かな実フローを決済するときの期待損失を「privacy subsidy」と定義する理論研究である。単一期間 Kyle、Glosten–Milgrom、連続時間 Kyle–Back に適用し、手数料による回収、取引量減少に伴う死荷重、内生的な privacy noise の選択まで分析する。ただし一般不可能性定理には、残差共分散がゼロであることと決済フローの信号可測性を同一視する重大な論理的不備がある。

**主な結果:** 論文は、単一価格の場合の maker 利益を $\Pi_M=-\operatorname{Cov}(v-\mathbb{E}[v\mid S],y-\mathbb{E}[y\mid S])$ と表し、Kyle では subsidy が $\lambda\sigma_\varepsilon^2$、Glosten–Milgrom では $\mu\eta\Delta$、Kyle–Back では $\sigma_v\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\sqrt{\sigma_u^2+\sigma_\varepsilon^2}$ になると報告する。さらに、小さい privacy noise の下では gross subsidy と break\-even fee が $O(\sigma_\varepsilon^2)$、手数料による死荷重が $O(\sigma_\varepsilon^4)$ になると主張する。一方、一般モデルで共分散がゼロとなることから $y$ が $S$ に関して可測であるとは導けないため、「厳密な粗視化なら効率的価格とゼロ利益が必ず両立不能」という中心命題は追加の識別・正の共分散条件なしには成立しない。

**Summary:** This theoretical paper defines the “privacy subsidy” as the expected loss incurred when a price setter uses posterior\-mean pricing based on a coarsened order\-flow signal while settling finer true flow\. It applies the idea to single\-period Kyle, Glosten–Milgrom, and continuous\-time Kyle–Back models, and then studies fee recovery, volume\-distortion deadweight loss, and endogenous privacy\-noise choice\. However, the general impossibility theorem contains a major logical flaw by treating zero residual covariance as equivalent to measurability of settled flow with respect to the signal\.

**Main result:** The paper reports that, under single\-price execution, maker profit is $\Pi_M=-\operatorname{Cov}(v-\mathbb{E}[v\mid S],y-\mathbb{E}[y\mid S])$, with subsidies of $\lambda\sigma_\varepsilon^2$ in Kyle, $\mu\eta\Delta$ in Glosten–Milgrom, and $\sigma_v\sigma_\varepsilon^2/\sqrt{\sigma_u^2+\sigma_\varepsilon^2}$ in Kyle–Back\. It further claims that, for small privacy noise, the gross subsidy and break\-even fee are $O(\sigma_\varepsilon^2)$ while fee\-induced deadweight loss is $O(\sigma_\varepsilon^4)$\. However, zero covariance does not imply that $y$ is measurable with respect to $S$ in a general model, so the central claim that strict coarsening necessarily makes efficient pricing and zero profit incompatible does not hold without additional identification or positive\-covariance conditions\.

**Practical application:** The framework can serve as a design benchmark for estimating liquidity\-provider compensation fees at venues that settle true order quantities while updating prices only from DP\-noised aggregate flow\. In particular, the Kyle formula provides a simple approximation for a fee floor based on privacy noise, ordinary noise flow, and value uncertainty\. It does not apply to batch auctions or standard CFMMs where priced and settled flow coincide, and the reported BTC/USDT figures are hypothetical calibrations rather than empirical estimates\.

**Methodology:** The paper begins with a covariance decomposition based on the orthogonality of conditional\-expectation residuals\. It then derives closed\-form equilibria for a Gaussian linear Kyle model, a Glosten–Milgrom model with a binary symmetric channel, and a Kyle–Back model using Kalman filtering and HJB/Riccati arguments\. It combines a volume\-proportional fee with linear demand or CARA hedgers to obtain a Harberger triangle, and adds a Gaussian differential\-privacy benefit to solve for an optimal noise scale\. The analysis is entirely theoretical and supported by symbolic calculations, with no estimation or testing on market data\.

**Limitations:** The principal problem is the generally false assertion that $\operatorname{Cov}(v-\mathbb{E}[v\mid S],y-\mathbb{E}[y\mid S])=0$ is equivalent to measurability of $y$ with respect to $S$\. Value and flow residuals can be orthogonal under strict coarsening, allowing zero maker profit; the general impossibility therefore holds only when positive residual covariance is imposed separately\. In the Glosten–Milgrom model, implementation is insufficiently explained because a maker observing only noisy direction nevertheless executes on the side associated with true direction, leaving the information set and executed price unclear\. The continuous\-time equilibrium closes a singular linear\-control HJB through an information\-budget constraint without a sufficiently rigorous verification of optimality and admissibility\. The fee and welfare results are small\-noise partial\-equilibrium approximations that hold price impact fixed, and the full fee\-volume equilibrium remains unsolved\. The paper also omits cryptographic implementation costs, inventory risk, competition, multiple insiders, endogenous or flow\-correlated noise, sequential Glosten–Milgrom dynamics, and empirical validation\.

**Why read it:** The idea of treating the mismatch between the pricing signal and settled flow as an explicit P&amp;L object is valuable, as is the attempt to unify several canonical microstructure models\. Nevertheless, the necessary\-and\-sufficient condition in the central general theorem is invalid, and unresolved consistency issues remain in the implementation and continuous\-time equilibrium\. The paper is therefore not recommended as an established general result in its current form; it requires revision to a conditional theorem with explicit positive residual covariance and renewed verification of each model\.

**Summary:** The paper studies market making under regime\-switching directional order flow and compares GLFT, a Rainbow/C51 DQN, and regime\-aware reinforcement\-learning policies in a zero\-intelligence LOB simulator with FIFO queue tracking\. It identifies inventory saturation as the principal failure mode of policies trained under stationary flow and proposes robustness enhancements based on a Bayesian change\-point belief, queue\-adjusted quote exposure, and scenario\-bandit fine\-tuning\.

**Main result:** In the authors' simulations, the RL policy outperformed GLFT across the entire observed risk\-return range under stationary symmetric flow\. With a mean regime duration of 60 MO events, the non\-adaptive policy's mean terminal PnL fell to about $-46\%$ of the stationary benchmark, while the regime\-aware Algorithm B recovered to about $37\%$ and reduced inventory dispersion from $1.89$ times to $0.55$ times the benchmark\. Algorithm C reportedly improved mean terminal PnL over Algorithm B by about $16\%$ and $17\%$ under random\-persistence and correlated\-direction stress, respectively\.

**Practical application:** An electronic market maker could incorporate change\-point estimates of market\-order direction and its own FIFO queue exposure into the state, adjusting quote skew in response to persistent one\-sided flow\. Scenario reweighting can also serve as a stress\-training method that focuses learning on adverse order\-flow paths rather than average performance alone\.

**Methodology:** The authors calibrate a Santa Fe LOB from AMZN Level\-3 data and configure GLFT using censored waiting\-time estimation and a volatility signature plot\. GLFT, an at\-best policy, and a Rainbow/C51 DQN are compared with paired seeds in the same queue\-aware event\-driven simulator\. For non\-stationary experiments, the buy\-MO probability is piecewise constant; a Bayesian online change\-point filter and queue\-adjusted quote\-exposure imbalance are added to the state to fine\-tune Algorithm B\. Algorithm C is then trained with a bandit over 256 stress scenarios that increases the sampling probability of low\-return scenarios\.

**Limitations:** All results come from a calibrated zero\-intelligence simulator, without historical\-market backtesting or live\-execution validation\. The model excludes strategic participants, endogenous order\-flow responses to the policy, latency, fees, order sizing, and multi\-level quoting, and permits only one unit\-lot order per side\. The regime width is fixed at $0.30$, durations are primarily exponential, and heavy tails and intraday seasonality are not tested\. Algorithm C is evaluated on only 50 episodes per stress family, and those stresses come from the same types of scenario generators used in training, so generalization to unknown real\-market distributions is not established\. Accordingly, the reported superiority over GLFT and profitability are conditional simulation findings\.

**Why read it:** The paper is useful for electronic\-execution research because it clearly connects non\-stationary order flow, FIFO queues, and inventory saturation, while providing a coherent comparison from analytical benchmarks through regime\-aware RL and tail\-focused fine\-tuning\. Because the evidence is centered on a single zero\-intelligence simulator, it should be read as a methodological study rather than evidence of production effectiveness\.
