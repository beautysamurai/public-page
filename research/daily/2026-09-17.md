**主な結果:** 論文は、bounded terminal data、Lipschitz・quadratic growth 条件の下で有限期間 quadratic G\-BSDE の存在、一意性、比較、安定性、BMO 評価を報告する。backward variable に strict monotonicity がある場合には一意な有界無限期間解を構成し、有限期間解との差がコンパクトな時間区間上で $C e^{-\mu(T-s)}$ により抑えられる。応用では、補償対数価格が指定プロファイル $\phi(X_s)$ に収束し、漸近利回りが所与の $\lambda$ になるフィードバック係数を構成する。

**実務への応用:** volatility ambiguity の下で、ボンド価格を参照して金利を調整するルールの自己整合的評価や、固定満期の価格目標および長期利回り目標から discount\-rate coefficients を逆算するための理論的基盤となる。ただし、実務的な政策ルールや取引モデルとして使うには、共通短期金利による満期間整合性、係数制約、推定、数値解法の追加が必要である。

**手法:** G\-expectation と G\-Brownian motion を用い、暗黙的な割引期待式を対数変換して quadratic G\-BSDE に帰着する。G\-BMO、Girsanov 型変換、線形化、比較原理、切断近似、Markovian regularity、viscosity PDE 表現を利用して有限期間理論を構築し、strict monotonicity と有限期間近似から無限期間解と horizon stability を導く。逆設計では G\-Itô dynamics を目標価格に一致させ、線形復元項 $\mu(y-\phi(x))$ を導入する。

It is a theoretical study of unique bounded finite\-horizon valuation, infinite\-horizon solutions, exponential convergence in maturity, and inverse design of rate coefficients that implement target prices and long\-term yields\.

**Main result:** The paper reports existence, uniqueness, comparison, stability, and BMO estimates for finite\-horizon quadratic G\-BSDEs under bounded terminal data, Lipschitz conditions, and quadratic\-growth assumptions\. With strict monotonicity in the backward variable, it constructs a unique bounded infinite\-horizon solution and bounds the difference from finite\-horizon solutions on compact time intervals by $C e^{-\mu(T-s)}$\. In its application, it constructs feedback coefficients under which the compensated logarithmic price converges to a prescribed profile $\phi(X_s)$ and the asymptotic yield equals a given $\lambda$\.

**Practical application:** The results provide a theoretical foundation for self\-consistent valuation of rules that adjust rates in response to bond prices under volatility ambiguity, and for deriving discount\-rate coefficients from fixed\-maturity price targets or long\-term yield targets\. Practical use as a policy rule or trading model would additionally require cross\-maturity consistency under a common short rate, coefficient constraints, estimation, and numerical methods\.

**Methodology:** The paper uses G\-expectation and G\-Brownian motion, applying a logarithmic transformation to reduce the implicit discounting equation to a quadratic G\-BSDE\. It develops the finite\-horizon theory using G\-BMO methods, Girsanov\-type transformations, linearization, comparison principles, truncation approximations, Markovian regularity, and a viscosity\-PDE representation\. Strict monotonicity and finite\-horizon approximation then yield the infinite\-horizon solution and horizon stability\. For inverse design, it matches G\-Itô dynamics to the target price and introduces the linear restoring term $\mu(y-\phi(x))$\.

**Limitations:** The results depend on strong assumptions including boundedness, Lipschitz continuity, strong ellipticity, quadratic\-growth control, and, on the infinite horizon, strict monotonicity\. The risk\-neutral sublinear expectation and no\-arbitrage condition based on symmetric G\-martingales are assumed exogenously; general equilibrium, welfare optimality, price formation, and market liquidity are not addressed\. Valuation centers on a single reference maturity, while simultaneous consistency across maturities under a common money\-market account remains unresolved\. Neither $\lambda$ nor the volatility\-uncertainty set is estimated, and the paper provides no empirical analysis, calibration, or numerical experiments\. The text also contains several notational inconsistencies involving indices and integration intervals, so the original proofs require careful verification before technical implementation\.

**Why read it:** The paper is directly relevant to interest\-rate and yield\-curve research and is novel in combining price\-dependent discounting with volatility ambiguity in one quadratic G\-BSDE framework\. Its finite\- and infinite\-horizon well\-posedness results and maturity\-direction convergence bound have substantial theoretical value\. Because the model remains abstract and lacks cross\-maturity consistency, estimation, and computational methods, it is recommended primarily for mathematical\-finance researchers rather than for immediate practical implementation\.
