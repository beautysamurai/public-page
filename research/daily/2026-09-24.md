## 1. Feasible Multi\-Asset Optimal Execution under Cash Constraints

**要約:** 本稿は、Almgren–Chriss型のmulti\-asset optimal executionに各時点の期待累積支出上限を導入し、売却代金で買付を賄うcash\-feasibleな執行計画を設計する。著者らは線形market impactの下で問題をconvex QCQPとして表現し、合成実験とagent\-based market simulatorで評価している。

**主な結果:** 著者らの定式化では、非負のimpact係数とrisk\-aversionの下で目的関数と期待cash制約が凸二次形式となり、global optimumを凸最適化で求められる。シミュレーションでは、制約を厳しくすると売りが先行し買いが後ろ倒しになった。基準数量の6資産ペアでは、AC freeのpeak cash drawdownが57\.06%～88\.45%だったのに対し、提案法は0\.03%～11\.81%と報告され、combined ISも全ペアでAC freeをわずかに下回った。ただしTWAPやVWAPがISで優れるペアもある。

**実務への応用:** 資金枠が限られるportfolio rebalancingで、各時点の期待資金使用量を明示的に管理しながら、複数銘柄の買いと売りのタイミングを共同最適化できる。既存のconvex solverを利用でき、運用会社のpre\-trade planningやfunding\-limit管理に適用しやすい。

**手法:** 線形のpermanent impactとtemporary impactを持つ離散時間Almgren–Chrissモデルを用い、expected implementation shortfallとその分散の加重和を最小化する。銘柄別の完了制約と各期間の期待累積cash上限を課し、目的関数と制約をQCQPへ構成的に変換して凸性を証明した。2銘柄のoracle実験に加え、3銘柄から成る6通りのbuy–sellペアを対象に、別日のagent\-based simulationからOLSでimpactを推定し、300回のvalidation simulationでTWAP、VWAP、cash制約なしのAC freeと比較した。

**限界:** cash制約は期待値にしか課されず、実現支出が予算を超える可能性がある。モデルは線形impact、独立な価格ショック、銘柄別impactを仮定し、cross\-impactや資産間共分散、非線形impact、出来高・参加率制約を扱わない。検証は実市場約定ではなく単一のagent\-based simulator、3銘柄、2銘柄ペア、限られた日付に依存する。impact推定のcalibration runも少なく、推定数量より大幅に大きい執行への外挿が含まれる。TWAP・VWAPに対するISの一貫した優位性はなく、期待制約から実現drawdownへの効果に関する一般化には追加検証が必要である。

**推奨理由:** 資金制約をpathwiseな運用課題としてoptimal executionに組み込み、標準的仮定の下で解釈可能なconvex QCQPに落とした点は、electronic executionの実務と研究の双方に有用である。一方、実証は合成市場に限定され、期待cash制約は実現ベースの資金安全性を保証しないため、現時点では実運用の完成形よりも有望な理論的benchmarkとして読むべきである。

**Summary:** The paper introduces per\-period limits on expected cumulative cash expenditure into Almgren–Chriss\-style multi\-asset optimal execution, seeking cash\-feasible schedules in which sale proceeds finance purchases\. Under linear market impact, the authors represent the problem as a convex QCQP and evaluate it using synthetic experiments and an agent\-based market simulator\.

**Main result:** In the authors’ formulation, the objective and expected\-cash constraints are convex quadratic forms when impact coefficients and risk aversion are nonnegative, allowing a global optimum to be obtained by convex optimization\. In simulations, tighter constraints moved sales earlier and delayed purchases\. Across six asset pairs at the benchmark size, peak cash drawdown was reported at 57\.06%–88\.45% for unconstrained AC versus 0\.03%–11\.81% for the proposed method; combined IS was also slightly lower than unconstrained AC for every pair\. TWAP or VWAP nevertheless achieved lower IS for some pairs\.

**Practical application:** The method can jointly optimize the timing of purchases and sales in capital\-limited portfolio rebalancing while explicitly controlling expected funding usage at each period\. It can use existing convex solvers and is potentially suitable for institutional pre\-trade planning and funding\-limit management\.

**Methodology:** The study uses a discrete\-time Almgren–Chriss model with linear permanent and temporary impact and minimizes a weighted sum of expected implementation shortfall and its variance\. It imposes asset\-level completion constraints and per\-period expected cumulative cash limits, constructively converts the objective and constraints into a QCQP, and proves convexity\. In addition to a two\-asset oracle experiment, it studies six ordered buy–sell pairs formed from three assets, estimates impact by OLS from a separate day of agent\-based simulations, and compares against TWAP, VWAP, and unconstrained AC over 300 validation simulations\.

**Limitations:** The cash constraint applies only in expectation, so realized expenditure can exceed the budget\. The model assumes linear impact, independent price shocks, and asset\-specific impact, omitting cross\-impact, inter\-asset covariance, nonlinear impact, and volume or participation constraints\. Validation relies on one agent\-based simulator, three assets, two\-asset pairs, and a limited set of dates rather than actual market executions\. The impact calibration uses few runs and includes extrapolation to execution sizes far above the calibration quantities\. IS does not consistently outperform TWAP or VWAP, and further evidence is needed before generalizing the realized\-drawdown benefits of an expected\-cash constraint\.

**Why read it:** Embedding funding limits as an intertemporal operational constraint and reducing the resulting execution problem to an interpretable convex QCQP is useful for both electronic\-execution research and practice\. However, because the evidence is limited to a synthetic market and expected\-cash constraints do not guarantee realized funding safety, the work is best viewed as a promising theoretical benchmark rather than a production\-ready solution\.
