Exploration annealing alone reduced DDQL's never\-trade collapses from 12 out of 100 runs to zero, but combining it with the reward modification restored collapse in 19 out of 30 runs\. No collapse was observed for MoE with $K=8$ under any of the six tested specifications\.

**Practical application:** The study is useful for designing validation procedures for RL execution models\. It highlights the need for many seeds, capacity\-matched comparisons, simple execution benchmarks, within\-policy CVaR, and checks of reward–exploration interactions\. Results exceeding TWAP are also reported after exploration annealing, but the authors explicitly interpret them as simulator diagnostics rather than evidence of live execution superiority\.

**Methodology:** Binance\-derived ten\-level order book data from January 2023 were aggregated into five\-minute means, with temporally separated training and evaluation periods\. In an environment selling 20 BTC over ten steps, DDQL, K\-means\-partitioned MoE, and capacity\-matched single networks were each trained 100 times and compared on 500 common evaluation windows\. Bootstrap, permutation, and collapse\-rate tests were used\. CPU\-based specification decomposition modified reward, exploration annealing, and replay retention using 30 or 100 seeds\.

**Limitations:** The study uses short\-period historical replay for one instrument without modelling trade feedback on prices or books\. Averaged books, nearly costless fills beyond visible depth, and an artificial terminal penalty that skips better quotes favour early liquidation\. Lagged routing across independently started episodes cannot identify the current market regime\. Confidence intervals capture training\-seed uncertainty conditional on 500 fixed windows, not uncertainty across market periods\. The dispersion reduction is not significant after multiple\-comparison adjustment, and test families were defined retrospectively\. Specification decomposition is incomplete, some arms use only 30 seeds, and individual outcomes differ between CPU and GPU\.

**Why read it:** Directly relevant to electronic execution, the paper provides useful negative results that help prevent conflating training reproducibility with execution tail risk\. It is recommended as research on RL execution evaluation design and failure diagnosis, not as evidence of a practical MoE advantage\.
