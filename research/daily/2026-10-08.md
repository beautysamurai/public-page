**Summary:** This theoretical paper reassesses Bonart's explanation of the square\-root impact law through price diffusivity\. The authors argue that additive growth of impact variance alone cannot establish the square\-root shape of mean impact or its participation\-independent amplitude\.

**Main result:** In an LMF\-type linear model, prices can be diffusive while an isolated metaorder has impact $I\propto\phi^{(1-\gamma)/2}Q^{(1+\gamma)/2}$\. In the proposed participant\-specific model, a participation\-independent square\-root law requires a perceived correlation plateau $C^{*}\propto\phi$\. The authors also show that volume conservation and variance accounting select $\delta=1/2$, conditional on mean impact carrying a finite share of daily variance and its coefficient being independent of the metaorder count\.

**Practical application:** Execution\-cost models should separately validate mean impact, participation dependence, and post\-execution decay rather than merely reproduce price diffusivity\. Comparing metaorders within stationary trading streams with temporally isolated metaorders can help test explanations based on stream whitening\.

**Methodology:** The paper examines an impact decomposition using counterfactual prices, an analytical LMF counterexample, participant\-specific adaptive propagators, detection thresholds from trade signs, long\-run all\-pass filter responses, and variance accounting based on volume conservation\. Independent, information\-neutral trading streams are central assumptions\.

**Limitations:** There is no new systematic empirical validation; the reference to CFM data suggesting that isolated orders also follow the square\-root law remains an unverified observation\. The prediction that isolated orders should violate the law applies only to the excursion interpretation of trading streams\. Detection thresholds concern estimators focused on trade signs and do not rule out detection using other market information\. The volume argument assumes independence, variance\-budget closure, and a finite variance contribution from mean impact, whose microscopic explanation remains unresolved\. The all\-pass critique also depends on a particular linear\-filter construction\.

**Why read it:** The paper is important for distinguishing the law's shape from its amplitude and mean impact from variance, thereby clarifying execution\-model validation requirements\. However, it does not empirically establish an alternative mechanical explanation\.
