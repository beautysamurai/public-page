**Summary:** This proof of concept uses a PFN to estimate causal market impact under confounding, where informed order flow drives both prices and trading volume\. It trains on synthetic markets and tests transfer to ABIDES, cryptocurrency perpetuals, and Nasdaq equities\. Causal identification is supplied by the training market prior, not observational data alone\.

**Main result:** In synthetic markets, the paper reports up to 68% overestimation by naive regression, while the transformer PFN achieves bias close to a true\-parameter reference model under strong confounding\. Across 1,000 held\-out ABIDES episodes, the prediction difference attributable to intervention inputs is 0\.38 pre\-onset standard deviations, matching the approximate average scale of counterfactual impact\. The recurrent variant improves correlation with 12 real\-data cell means from 0\.68 to 0\.92–0\.96, but weakens synthetic\-market deconfounding\.

**Practical application:** The framework is useful as a research platform for separating information\-driven price changes from trading effects in execution\-cost estimation\. Deployment requires encoding orders as added flow rather than a price clamp, and calibrating units, domain\-specific scale, and prediction intervals\. It is currently better suited to a validation prototype than a production execution\-optimization model\.

**Methodology:** A continuous\-time linear stochastic model of hidden informational flow, observable order flow, and price generates training data with controlled confounding\. Evaluation combines quantile learning on shared\-noise intervention/no\-intervention pairs, matched models without intervention inputs, and classical impact baselines\. ABIDES supplies counterfactuals through same\-seed replay; real\-market tests evaluate factual price predictions around detected flow bursts and aggregate impact\.

**Limitations:** Causal effects under hidden confounding are not identified from observational data alone, so results depend on prior assumptions\. Real\-market bursts have unverified exogeneity, and reduced prediction bias does not prove causal transfer\. On real data, the square\-root law outperforms the transformer, while the recurrent model's high correlation concerns cell means rather than individual orders\. Under zero\-shot transfer, nominal 80% interval coverage falls to 32% in ABIDES, requiring recalibration\. Comparisons that change architecture and training recipe together, domain\-specific scale, and sensitivity to incorrect intervention labels also constrain practical use\.

**Why read it:** The paper directly addresses the site's core themes by testing causal market\-impact estimation using analytic confounding bias, intervention\-input ablations, and counterfactual simulation\. Real\-market causal identification and superiority over existing methods remain unestablished, but the findings on market priors and intervention encoding make it worth recommending\.

**Summary:** This empirical study examines single\-name FLEX activity in relation to regular listed\-option markets and underlying equities\. The full\-text evidence supports interpreting FLEX as an observable marker of institutional volatility demand and positioning, rather than a persistent stock\-return prediction signal\.

**Main result:** Matched DiD reports a positive coefficient for regular\-option log open interest \($\beta=0.3061$, BH\-adjusted $p=0.0168$\)\. This measures a sustained level difference after the first FLEX event, not a short\-window event effect\. ATM IV is higher among FLEX names in the mid\-price tier \($\beta=0.1235$, $p=0.0030$\), but the pooled result across tiers is insignificant\. Equity abnormal\-return differences appear only on the event day; the broad rich\-IV short\-straddle proxy is insignificant after costs and has a negative mean with a one\-trading\-day delay\.

**Practical application:** FLEX information may help monitor position formation and identify names warranting investigation for large gaps between implied and realized volatility\. It requires further validation using leg\-level quotes and information\-release timestamps, rather than direct adoption for directional trading or production deployment\.

**Methodology:** The study links FLEX records, regular\-option aggregates, equity prices, and corporate\-action adjustments over 250 trading days from March 31, 2025, to March 27, 2026\.
