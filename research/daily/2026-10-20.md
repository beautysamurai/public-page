**Summary:** The paper asks how to maximize a quantile of total sale proceeds, specified before trading begins, while adapting to observed prices\. Rather than reoptimizing VaR at the same level each period, the policy updates the remaining allowable shortfall probability after each observation\. The authors justify this approach for Markov decision processes on standard Borel spaces, including continuous states and actions, and derive optimal adaptive liquidation strategies in an Almgren–Chriss execution model\.

**Main result:** Let $\kappa$ be the permanent impact coefficient, $\eta$ the temporary impact coefficient, and $\tau$ the trading interval\. When $\kappa\geq 2\eta/\tau$, it is optimal to choose between selling everything and waiting\. With $N=2$ trading opportunities, adaptation offers no advantage\. For $N\geq 3$, it strictly improves on the best static strategy if and only if the revenue quantile level satisfies $\alpha&gt;4^{-(N-1)}\binom{2N-2}{N-1}$\. When $\kappa&lt;2\eta/\tau$, partial sales can be optimal\. The evidence for improvement in this regime is numerical: the authors report a maximum increase in the revenue quantile of 0\.37% over the static strategy in the base case and 1\.93% across all settings tested\.

**Practical application:** In the sell\-or\-wait regime, the trader can precompute the target quantile of the running maximum of standardized cumulative price innovations over the horizon\. Sell the entire position at the first threshold crossing, checking from the start of trading, or at the deadline if the threshold is never reached\. In the partial\-sale regime, trade sizes can be precomputed by remaining inventory and risk level, with the risk level updated after each price observation\. Both policies provide benchmarks for measuring what adaptation to prices adds over a fixed schedule\.

**Methodology:** The authors prove a VaR decomposition that allocates allowable shortfall probabilities across next states while keeping their conditional average at or below the current level\.
