**Summary:** The paper proposes a benchmark for evaluating how generative limit order book models respond to exogenous metaorders\. Testing five neural generators across five NASDAQ stocks, it reports that distributional fidelity alone cannot establish suitability as an execution environment\.

**Main result:** Impact not explained by mechanical book changes alone was detected in all 25 model–stock pairs\. However, estimated exponents were $\delta \in [0.63,1.21]$, with every confidence interval excluding the square\-root exponent\. On AMD, amplitudes were approximately 4–17 times the literature benchmark, and no pair exhibited credible post\-execution relaxation under the drift\-aware assessment\.

**Practical application:** The benchmark is useful for validating simulated impact shape, amplitude, and relaxation before training execution algorithms or reinforcement\-learning agents\. It complements distributional tests by identifying environments that could teach unrealistic execution strategies despite matching price and order distributions\.

**Methodology:** Evaluation has three phases: free generation, metaorder injection, and post\-execution observation\. Median\-sized aggressive child orders target 10% participation, with 1,024 rollouts per direction per phase spread across 20 trading days\. Visible injections are compared with blind controls that execute orders without revealing them to the model; estimators are validated against known kernel controls, and intervals use trading\-day cluster bootstrapping\.

**Limitations:** Testing covers one month on one market, five stocks, and fixed participation, child size, and aggressive\-order settings; EA is outside the training universe\. Real\-market reference values come mainly from prior literature rather than matched empirical metaorders, and even known\-kernel controls fail the square\-root\-law criterion\. Short context length as the cause of missing relaxation remains a hypothesis, while long\-rollout drift and month\-to\-month variation further constrain interpretation\.

**Why read it:** The control design separating mechanical book consumption from generator responses is directly useful for execution\-simulator validation\. Although generalization is limited, the benchmark exposes practically important defects that distributional evaluation alone can miss\.

**Summary:** The paper examines how speculative imbalance positions held by non\-physical traders affect balancing volumes and settlement costs in Great Britain’s electricity market\. Counterfactual analysis using participant\-level data from 2021–2024 reports that these positions reduced balancing requirements and settlement risk\.

**Main result:** Relative to a counterfactual without speculative positions, cumulative absolute net balancing volume is estimated to be 3\.1–3\.9 TWh, or 16–20%, lower\. Balancing costs are £347–396 million lower, and suppliers’ imbalance settlement liabilities are £193–325 million lower\. However, the balancing\-cost effect reverses in one scenario for 2022\.

**Practical application:** The framework can assess how arbitrage affects balancing markets when evaluating single imbalance pricing or changes to trading deadlines\. A useful insight is to compare outcomes against the system balance without speculation rather than judging speculation solely by its alignment with the realized system balance\.

**Methodology:** The study constructs counterfactuals removing aggregate non\-physical\-trader imbalances across 68,823 half\-hour settlement periods after excluding missing observations\. It compares AMV, covering all eligible traders, with ZMV, restricted to traders with zero metered volume, and recalculates balancing costs, settlement prices, and participant settlement cashflows through merit\-order redispatch while holding constraint\-management actions fixed\.

**Limitations:** Other participants’ imbalances, balancing\-market bids, and constraint\-management actions are held fixed, making the results model\-dependent rather than directly identified causal effects\. **Why read it:** The paper merits recommendation as market\-microstructure research because it uses empirical data to examine interactions between arbitrage participants and settlement rules\.
