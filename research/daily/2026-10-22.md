**Summary:** The paper analyzes LP range selection and rebalancing in concentrated\-liquidity DEXs as a mean\-field game with competition for fees\. It distinguishes general equilibrium theory from profitability analysis of strategies that rebalance at range boundaries\.

**Main result:** The averaged game admits an equilibrium with unique aggregate liquidity and value function\. The stationary model restricted to boundary rebalancing yields critical volatility $\sigma_c^2=4f\bar V/(\sqrt{\bar q}\bar L)$, an approximate optimal width, and free\-entry conditions\. For the Ethereum 0\.3% pool, the paper reports $\sigma/\sigma_c=0.983$ with a 95% confidence interval of $[0.945,1.023]$, but this does not establish profitability or causally identify free entry\.

**Practical application:** Fees, volume, and active\-tick liquidity can help screen LP profitability and analyze the sensitivity of range widths and minimum entry capital to rebalancing costs\. Entry decisions depend on strategy and cost assumptions, however, and are not directly actionable trading signals\.

**Methodology:** The paper formulates finite\-state continuous\-time Markov control through an HJB–KFE system and analyzes equilibrium using a fixed point and fee\-dilution monotonicity\. Rebalancing at the actual price is analyzed separately through an offset process and renewal\-reward methods, with hourly data and block\-bootstrap evaluation across five WETH/USDC pools from January 2024 to July 2026\.

**Limitations:** No limit theorem connects the averaged game to actual rebalancing, so the general existence and uniqueness results cannot be transferred to the latter\. Stationary solutions depend on boundary rebalancing, fast execution, exogenous prices and order flow, and risk neutrality\. The driftless log\-price assumption is important: making the price a martingale removes the strict entry cutoff\. Arbitrage alone can explain the observed ratio, several pools exceed the theoretical cutoff, and backtests still incur losses\. Jump overshoots and uncertain cost assumptions limit the precision of capital thresholds\.

**Why read it:** Recommended for connecting DEX fee competition and rebalancing costs in a tractable framework with an observable profitability indicator\. The evidence does not establish the equilibrium mechanism or trading profits, so the paper is best read as foundational research for strategy design\.
