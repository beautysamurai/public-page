**Summary:** The paper models market\-making executed volume as the product of average order size, an exponential demand function, and a Prony\-series multiplier using a state\-dependent Hawkes process\. It combines the volume model with quote placement through inventory\-constrained dynamic programming, but the empirical exercise uses future order flow retrospectively rather than validating an implementable trading strategy\.

**Main result:** For the MSFT evaluation day, the paper reports terminal utility of USD 10,460 for the model recalibrated under the inventory constraint, versus USD 8,559 for the constant\-multiplier baseline\. Parameters trained separately without the constraint produce a reported USD 119,707, but all these figures are model\-based evaluations conditional on the entire realized order\-flow path, not evidence of live trading profits\. The theoretical result establishes uniform approximation by finite Prony sums, not accuracy of the implemented two\-term approximation or global optimality\.

**Practical application:** The proposed volume model offers a design template for linking order flow and quote depth to existing quoting rules\. It also helps distinguish the roles of inventory limits and terminal penalties, but deployment requires reevaluation with a policy that excludes future information and an independent execution simulator\.

**Methodology:** MSFT order\-book data from November 2014 are split into four training days and one evaluation day\. Per\-second order counts are grouped into six bins for each side, and best\-quote imbalance into five states; 1,452 intensity parameters are fitted using approximate stochastic least\-squares updates\. **Summary:** The paper learns stratification factors with neural networks for Monte Carlo under proportional allocation\. It establishes continuity of conditional expectations and distributions under approximation of Gaussian conditioning factors, and tests the approach on a CMS range accrual in G2\+\+ and a worst\-of autocallable in a Wishart model\.

**Main result:** When deterministic integrands converge and factor covariance ranks are eventually preserved, the paper proves mean\-square convergence of conditional expectations and convergence in probability of conditional distributions in the weak topology\. Numerical experiments report variance\-reduction factors relative to crude Monte Carlo of up to 5\.448 for the CMS product and 2\.870 for the autocallable\.

**Practical application:** The approach can reduce standard errors without increasing the number of simulation paths when pricing path\-dependent CMS products in multifactor interest\-rate models\. However, reductions in elapsed time including training costs are not demonstrated\.

**Methodology:** Networks represent time\-dependent deterministic integrands, producing stratification factors as linear functionals of the driving Gaussian noise\. For the autocallable, increasing factor dimension from two to three does not improve realized variance reduction\.

**Why read it:** Although it does not improve the interest\-rate model itself, the paper directly addresses Monte Carlo valuation of CMS products and provides theoretical support for the stability of learned stratification factors\.
