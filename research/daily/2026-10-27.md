**Summary:** The paper distinguishes liquidity concentration at round prices from price persistence unexplained by depth\. Using five Nasdaq stocks in 2025, it analyzes departure durations and directions separately and reports that greater displayed liquidity does not imply additional persistence\.

**Main result:** Displayed liquidity concentrates at whole\-dollar prices in all five stocks, but there is no evidence of longer depth\-adjusted durations\. For Intel and SoFi, the main specification estimates adjusted mean durations approximately 6–7% shorter than at control levels, with departures biased away from whole\-dollar prices\. **Practical application:** The framework is useful for treating liquidity concentration, departure speed, and departure direction as separate features in execution and market\-making models\. It also illustrates how contemporaneous depth adjustment can absorb persistence caused by replenishment\. However, execution probabilities, queue position, and improvements in profitability after transaction costs are not tested\.

**Methodology:** The authors construct a price series updated at one\-tick\-spread moments and associated visits from Nasdaq ITCH best quotes\. For one\-tick departures, departure counts and occupation times yield $\widehat{\sigma}^{2}(k\delta)=\delta^{2}N_t^k/T_t^k$\. They use hazard regression with spline controls for entry bid and ask depths and a directional logit model, evaluated with day\-clustered standard errors, multiple\-testing adjustments, and queue\-dynamics simulations\.

**Limitations:** The sample covers five stocks on one venue over one year, and the results are descriptive rather than causal\. The main mean\-duration ratios are inverse hazard ratios under a constant within\-visit baseline assumption, leaving their interpretation model\-dependent when elapsed\-time dependence exists\. Apple is sensitive to observation exclusions and price\-series construction, while American Airlines has limited statistical power\. The exact theoretical occupation identity assumes one\-tick moves, whereas the empirical duration analysis also includes multi\-tick departures\. The referenced online appendices are not included in the supplied material\.

**Why read it:** Recommended for order\-book\-based execution research because it reassesses the interpretation of round prices as simple persistence barriers and concretely demonstrates nonlinear depth effects, internal\-covariate problems, and the distinction between hazard ratios and mean\-duration ratios\.
