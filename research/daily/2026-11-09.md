## 1. Drift\-invariant kernels for discount curve models

**要約:** 割引曲線のkernel ridge regression（KRR）が生成する有限次元アフィン空間について、リスク中立ドリフトに対する不変性を解析した理論研究。静的な曲線推定と連続時間の金利モデルを整合させるカーネルを特徴付ける。

**主な結果:** 著者らは、RKHSの次元が高々2の例外的な場合を除き、すべてのキャッシュフロー日程でドリフト不変となるカーネルが $k(x,y)=e^{b(x+y)}(ce^{axy}+1-c)$、$a,c&gt;0$、$b\in\mathbb{R}$ に限られると証明する。推定曲線は一定利回りの割引曲線のアフィン結合となり、固定した事前曲線を共有する不変部分空間は日程の部分集合に対応する。係数のリスク中立ドリフトは二次式だが、割引後の係数では線形になる。

**実務への応用:** 曲線推定と動的金利モデルで共通の曲線族を使う際のカーネル選択に役立つ。対象空間の次元削減ではPCAではなく部分日程の選択が必要となり、割引後の係数にボラティリティを設定するモデル構築の指針も得られる。

**手法:** Representer theoremと原点で条件付けたRKHSを用いてKRRの推定空間を導く。無裁定ドリフト $\eta(f)=f'-f'(0)f$ の不変性を線形作用素の不変部分空間問題へ還元し、カーネルの対称性・正半定値性と解析性から分類する。Itô計算により係数ダイナミクスとlinear\-rationalモデルへの接続を導く。

**限界:** 実証評価や推定精度・次元削減手法の比較は行っていない。すべての日程に対する連続時間の不変性は強い条件で、固定日程や離散時間での弱い整合性は未解決である。ドリフト不変性だけでは正の債券価格や大域解は保証されず、正の状態価格密度などの追加条件が必要となる。小さいカーネルパラメータによる悪条件化や長期端の有界性にも制約がある。

**推奨理由:** 割引曲線の機械学習推定と無裁定ダイナミクスの整合性を必要十分条件で整理しており、金利モデル設計に直接関係する。即時の運用導入より、カーネル選択とモデル構造の理論的検証に有用である。

**Summary:** This theoretical paper studies invariance under the risk\-neutral drift of finite\-dimensional affine spaces generated by kernel ridge regression \(KRR\) for discount curves\. It characterizes kernels that reconcile static curve estimation with continuous\-time interest\-rate models\.

**Main result:** The authors prove that, apart from exceptional cases with RKHS dimension at most two, the kernels that are drift\-invariant for every cash\-flow grid are exactly $k(x,y)=e^{b(x+y)}(ce^{axy}+1-c)$, with $a,c&gt;0$ and $b\in\mathbb{R}$\. Estimated curves are affine combinations of constant\-yield discount curves, and invariant subspaces sharing the fixed prior curve correspond to subsets of the grid\. The coefficients have quadratic risk\-neutral drift, while their discounted counterparts have linear drift\.

**Practical application:** The results guide kernel selection when curve estimation and dynamic interest\-rate modeling must share a curve family\. Dimension reduction within the specified space requires selecting sub\-grids rather than PCA, and the paper also guides model construction by specifying volatility for discounted coefficients\.

**Methodology:** The representer theorem and an RKHS conditioned at the origin establish the KRR hypothesis space\. Invariance under the no\-arbitrage drift $\eta(f)=f'-f'(0)f$ is reduced to an invariant\-subspace problem for a linear operator; kernel symmetry, positive semidefiniteness, and analyticity yield the classification\. Itô calculus establishes coefficient dynamics and the connection to linear\-rational models\.

**Limitations:** The paper provides no empirical evaluation or comparison of estimation accuracy and dimension\-reduction methods\. Continuous\-time invariance for every grid is restrictive; weaker consistency for a fixed grid or discrete time remains unresolved\. Drift\-invariance alone guarantees neither positive bond prices nor global solutions; additional conditions, including a positive state price density, are needed\. Small kernel parameters can cause ill\-conditioning, and long\-end boundedness imposes further restrictions\.

**Why read it:** The paper provides necessary and sufficient conditions linking machine\-learning discount\-curve estimation with arbitrage\-free dynamics, making it directly relevant to interest\-rate model design\. Its main value is theoretical validation of kernel choices and model structure rather than immediate operational deployment\.

**Summary:** The paper tests whether normalization based on microstructure invariance improves cross\-asset and cross\-market transfer of reinforcement\-learning execution policies\. Policies trained on 954 Nasdaq stocks in 2019 are applied without retraining to unseen stocks, later years, Coinbase, and IEX\. The central implication is that recent trading statistics and tick information help within familiar market scales, but a small transfer gap alone does not establish representation quality\.

**Main result:** The authors report that cost differences between MS\+tick and a comparator whose training included the target stock fall within an equivalence margin of $0.015$ mean spreads per share in both test periods\. However, this comparison covers only subsets of stocks with historical data\. Normalization's mean\-cost advantage is small; exploratory analysis finds that a common reward scale and window statistics as inputs remove most of raw features' disadvantage\. Extrapolation to unseen spread regimes fails, and raw features sometimes perform equally well or better after fees or at the median\.

**Practical application:** The findings inform shared make\-or\-take policies across stocks\. Using the preceding 30 minutes' intertrade times, trade sizes, spreads, and tick information, with a common reward scale, may reduce dependence on per\-stock training\. Deployment should check training coverage of spread regimes, net\-of\-fee costs, and tail risk, and compare costs across representations and both stock\- and episode\-weighted aggregates rather than relying only on transfer gaps\.

**Methodology:** A non\-intrusive order\-by\-order replay tracks queue position and latency, allowing waiting, limit\-order changes, cancellation, and immediate execution at ten decision times\. The main learner is tree\-based fitted Q\-iteration, with neural fitted Q\-iteration and PPO checks\. Invariant units, raw features, and standard normalizations are compared on identical paths using bootstrap intervals and prespecified equivalence margins\. Theoretical propositions, synthetic order books, and pendulum experiments also examine the diagnostic power of transfer gaps\.

**Limitations:** The replay covers only one venue's displayed book, excluding market impact, other participants' reactions, routing, order protection, and hidden liquidity\. Consequently, it can overstate crossing costs and policy differences; on IEX especially, an extreme episode and aggregation choices affect results\. The main objective is mean cost before fees, public sample days are sparse, and crypto coverage is limited to 16 pairs\. Zero\-shot deployment still requires 30 minutes of recent target data\. Many additional analyses are exploratory, and assumptions such as smoothness in the pooled\-comparator theory do not hold for the main tree learner, making that application heuristic\.

**Why read it:** Broad stock\-level tests and controlled experiments concretely illustrate both shared execution\-policy design and pitfalls in transfer evaluation, making the paper directly relevant to electronic execution\. It is valuable as research on normalization and evaluation design, not as proof of live trading cost savings\.
